I listened to a great podcast recently with [James Cowling on The Peterman Pod](https://open.spotify.com/episode/1Xt2SfdlM8huFQERnsknqP?si=NaAKQpXaS2eclbg5RRc1TA) and one of the topics James talks about is the concept of leading by example, specifically why you shouldn’t.

I’ve been a tech lead and now a Principal Engineer and I’ve made my own mistakes during that time but one thing I’ve valued has been my ability to lead by example. 

In a hybrid individual contributor / leadership role, it’s easy to get caught between the blurred lines of doing the work and helping others to their work. Even in that last sentence, now I know the difference between my work and others work, that wasn’t always so. 

## What I struggled with early on

As a tech lead, there were many times I wasn’t able to separate myself from the work, in that even if someone else wrote the code, I felt responsible for it whether in success or failure. This often culminated in over prescribing how something should be implemented, or arguing over pull request comments about minor details. 
One day I just had to let go and I thought that was the process playing itself out. 

By letting go, it had the opposite effect, now the team felt like they were building with another team member, not a leader.

However, as time went on I now know my opinion is often the result of some experience that can be shared with someone else - whether or not they choose to accept it. 

## The traps we fall into

**Thinking everyone is watching you**
Everyone else is busy with their own work, it actually takes a lot of effort to make people stop what they’re doing and listen to you. 

**Always being available to help**
Perhaps most detrimental to your own work in the long term, if you keep focussing on being the problem solver, no one else gets to sharpen their skills and share the load.

**Fire fighting reduces your strategic capacity**
Constantly resolving bugs or product issues as they come up reduces the amount of time spent on strategic problems. The big system architecture will never see the light of day if you’re too busy solving problems of today.

## Aspiring to lead by example

**Showing enthusiasm for the problem and the customer**
Engineers are notoriously problem solvers, but it takes a great engineer to solve the right problem. 

**Solving problems for your users**
Product engineering is often the tradeoff between the best user experience and how to cost effectively provide it for all users.Sometimes these are at odds with each other. 

**Showing vulnerability in learning**
Too often we see leaders as infallible humans who can do no wrong but the leaders I respect the most are ones who admit when something went wrong and learn the most from it.  

## Summary
Leading by example used to mean being the best version of myself at all times, showing up when others wouldn’t. In a lot of ways I resonated with James’ original post on the subject.
I used to have to know the answer to every question, solution to every problem and be able to come up with it on the spot.
