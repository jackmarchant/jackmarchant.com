I listened to a great podcast recently with [James Cowling on The Peterman Pod](https://open.spotify.com/episode/1Xt2SfdlM8huFQERnsknqP?si=NaAKQpXaS2eclbg5RRc1TA) and one of the topics James talks about is the concept of leading by example, specifically why you shouldn’t.

I’ve been a tech lead and now a Principal Engineer and I’ve made my own mistakes during that time but one thing I thought I valued has been my ability to lead by example. 

In a hybrid individual contributor / leadership role, it’s easy to get caught between the blurred lines of doing the work and helping others to their work. Even in that last sentence, now I know the difference between my work and others work, that wasn’t always the case.

## What I struggled with early on

As a tech lead, there were times I wasn’t able to separate myself from the work, in that even if someone else wrote the code, I felt responsible for it either in success or failure. 
I really found it difficult to let go of the minor details or even to make the distinction between what should be in or out of my control.
One day I just had to let go and I thought that was the process playing itself out. 

By letting go, it had the opposite effect: the team felt like they were building with another team member, not a leader steering people in the right direction.

However, as time went on, I realised my opinion is the result of an experience where I learned a valuable lesson. That lesson can be shared and can help others in the moment.

## The traps we fall into

These are some realisations I made about leading by example, in how I defined it.

**Thinking everyone is watching you**

Everyone is busy with their own work, it actually takes a lot of effort to make people stop and listen to you. 

**Always being available to help**

Perhaps most detrimental to your own work in the long term, if you keep focussing on being the problem solver, no one else gets to sharpen their skills and share the load.

**Fire fighting reduces your strategic capacity**

Constantly resolving bugs or product issues as they come up reduces the amount of time spent on strategic problems. The big system architecture will never see the light of day if you’re too busy solving problems of today.

## Aspiring to lead by example

These are the things I value now about leading by example.

**Showing enthusiasm for the problem and empathy for the customer**

Engineers are notoriously problem solvers, but it takes a great engineer to solve the right problem and feel it from the customer's perspective.

**Solving problems for your users**

Product engineering is often the tradeoff between the best user experience and how to cost effectively provide it for all users. Sometimes these are at odds with each other. 

**Showing vulnerability in learning**

Too often we see leaders as infallible humans who can do no wrong but the leaders I respect the most are ones who show a willingness to learn, from their own mistakes or from others.

## Summary
Leading by example to me used to mean I need to be on my game every day and never make mistakes, showing up when others wouldn’t. Saying yes when everyone expects you to say no.
In a lot of ways I resonated with [James’ original post on the subject](https://medium.com/@jamesacowling/dont-lead-by-example-4f86b1174e64).
I used to have to know the answer to every question, solution to every problem and be able to come up with it on the spot. 

Now, I see the value in figuring out the problem together and not trying to be the smartest person in the room with all the answers.

The example you can set for others is that we all want to do important work and feel proud of it.
